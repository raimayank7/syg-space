What is in the solar system?

At the centre is the Sun, a star that holds 99.8% of all the mass. Round it go eight planets. The four nearest, Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars, are small and rocky. The four beyond, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, are giants with rings and many moons. By NASA's count the eight planets have 416 moons between them. Pluto, once called the ninth planet, has been classed as a dwarf planet since 2006.

A sense of scale

Earth is 12,756 km across and 149.6 million km from the Sun. Jupiter is 11.2 times as wide as Earth. Neptune, the farthest planet, is 30 times as far from the Sun as we are, and sunlight takes 4.2 hours to reach it. The Moon, our nearest neighbour, is 384,000 km away.

Common questions

How many planets are there?

Eight: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006.

Which is the largest planet?

Jupiter. It is 142,984 km across, 11.2 times the width of Earth.

Which is the hottest planet?

Venus. NASA's fact sheet gives its mean temperature as 464 °C, hotter than Mercury although Mercury is nearer the Sun.
