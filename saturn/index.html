Saturn: facts and figures

Saturn by the numbers, from NASA's planetary fact sheet, with each figure compared with Earth.

About Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest planet in the solar system, famous for its rings. It has dozens of moons, among them Enceladus, which sprays jets of water, and smoggy Titan with its lakes of methane.

Source: NASA, read on 12 November 2026.

Saturn compared with Earth

Size. Saturn is 120,536 km across. That is 9.4 times the width of Earth (12,756 km). About 9.4 Earths would fit side by side across it. Its mass is 95.1 times Earth's.

Gravity and your weight. Gravity at its cloud tops is 9 m/s², against 9.8 on Earth. So things weigh 92% of what they do on Earth. Someone who weighs 50 kg on Earth would feel like 45.9 kg there. The giant planets have no solid surface to stand on, so the figure is for the level in the atmosphere where the pressure is 1 bar. Try your own weight in the weight calculator.

Day. Saturn turns once on its axis in 10.7 hours. From one noon to the next, its day lasts 10.7 hours.

Year. One trip round the Sun takes 10,747 Earth days, which is 29.43 Earth years. A ten-year-old on Earth would be 0.3 years old there, counting in that world's years. See the age calculator.

Distance. Saturn is on average 1,432 million km from the Sun, between 1,357.6 million km at its closest and 1,506.5 million km at its farthest. Sunlight takes 79.6 minutes to arrive. That is 9.57 times Earth's distance.

Tilt and temperature. Its axis is tilted by 26.7°. A tilt like this gives it seasons, as on Earth (23.4°). The mean temperature in the fact sheet is −140 °C.

Moons, rings and magnetism. The fact sheet counts 274 moons for Saturn. Rings: yes. A global magnetic field: yes.
