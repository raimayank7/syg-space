Jupiter: facts and figures

Jupiter by the numbers, from NASA's planetary fact sheet, with each figure compared with Earth.

About Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet. NASA says it has more than twice the combined material of the other bodies in the solar system apart from the Sun. Its stripes and swirls are cold, windy clouds of ammonia and water floating in an atmosphere of hydrogen and helium. The Great Red Spot is a giant storm, bigger than Earth, that has raged for hundreds of years.

Source: NASA, read on 12 November 2026.

Jupiter compared with Earth

Size. Jupiter is 142,984 km across. That is 11.2 times the width of Earth (12,756 km). About 11.2 Earths would fit side by side across it. Its mass is 317.9 times Earth's.

Gravity and your weight. Gravity at its cloud tops is 23.1 m/s², against 9.8 on Earth. So things weigh 236% of what they do on Earth. Someone who weighs 50 kg on Earth would feel like 117.9 kg there. The giant planets have no solid surface to stand on, so the figure is for the level in the atmosphere where the pressure is 1 bar. Try your own weight in the weight calculator.

Day. Jupiter turns once on its axis in 9.9 hours. From one noon to the next, its day lasts 9.9 hours.

Year. One trip round the Sun takes 4,331 Earth days, which is 11.86 Earth years. A ten-year-old on Earth would be 0.8 years old there, counting in that world's years. See the age calculator.

Distance. Jupiter is on average 778.5 million km from the Sun, between 740.6 million km at its closest and 816.4 million km at its farthest. Sunlight takes 43.3 minutes to arrive. That is 5.2 times Earth's distance.

Tilt and temperature. Its axis is tilted by 3.1°. It is so close to upright that it has almost no seasons. The mean temperature in the fact sheet is −110 °C.

Moons, rings and magnetism. The fact sheet counts 95 moons for Jupiter. Rings: yes. A global magnetic field: yes.
